# Sprint 12 GA-readiness delivery boundary

T-0404 begins planning for the historical Sprint 12 GA-launch work. It defines
the decisions and evidence a future GA-readiness proposal must have; it does
not approve, schedule, announce, deploy, activate, certify, or launch general
availability.

## Canonical context

The roadmap calls for a GA-readiness checklist, GraphQL rollout, SOC 2 controls
inventory, marketing and training material, API documentation, multi-region
groundwork, and a launch/onboarding event. The production-readiness source
defines gated evidence across operations, security, privacy, data protection,
reliability, and release governance.

Those roadmap deliverables and source checklist entries are planning inputs.
They do not establish a current GA date, scope, certification, audit schedule,
region, customer availability, commercial readiness, reliability result, or
production-service claim.

## Scope and non-goals

This task creates no GA checklist status, approval, launch date, release,
feature flag, rollout, tenant, customer, prospect, contract, price, invoice,
marketing page, training material, documentation publication, certification,
audit engagement, region, infrastructure, API, browser route, activation,
deployment configuration, announcement, or production-enablement claim.

It does not choose a launch market, customer segment, release scope, version,
region, availability objective, pricing, communications plan, auditor,
certification, launch date, or go/no-go threshold.

## Required approvals before a GA-readiness proposal

All of the following must be explicitly recorded before a future GA-readiness,
rollout, launch, customer-communication, or production-enablement task can be
proposed:

1. Founders, Product, and Engineering approve the proposed market, customer
   segment, release scope, dependency status, supported capabilities, known
   exclusions, launch criteria, rollback/hold behavior, and customer
   communication authority. A planned capability cannot be represented as
   available before its own release gates are complete.
2. Platform, SRE, and Security approve the future production-readiness,
   isolation, reliability, observability, incident response, backup/recovery,
   change-management, rollback, access, and evidence-review design. No
   production readiness is implied without approved evidence.
3. Privacy, Legal, Security, and Product approve the jurisdiction-specific
   privacy, data-protection, retention/erasure, contract, notice, processor,
   and customer-communication requirements. A controls inventory or draft pack
   is not a compliance, certification, or legal determination.
4. Finance, Legal, Product, and Customer Success approve the future commercial
   terms, pricing, invoicing/tax readiness, support boundaries, onboarding,
   training, escalation, and external communication. Planning prices and
   forecast assumptions are not executable offers.
5. AI, Privacy, Legal, Product, Security, and Platform confirm that every
   model or biometric capability in the proposed scope has satisfied its
   separate release and consent gates. GA readiness must not activate face
   attendance, enrollment, matching, liveness, a model, or another gated
   capability by implication.

## Future implementation constraints

- A future GA checklist must contain approved evidence references and owners
  only; it must not contain customer data, credentials, incident details,
  footage, biometric information, model artifacts, test results, or unbounded
  operational logs.
- A future rollout, flag, deployment, documentation publication, training,
  marketing communication, or customer onboarding path must be separately
  authorized, tenant-scoped where applicable, reversible, and auditable with
  safe result categories. It must not change customer access or publish an
  availability claim automatically.
- A future external assurance or controls-inventory activity must follow the
  approved Phase 10 security and compliance process. It must not assert SOC 2,
  another certification, legal compliance, or an audit result without the
  required human authority.
- A missing approval, unresolved release gate, unsafe operational state,
  unknown tenant boundary, ambiguous commercial/legal term, or unavailable
  rollback must hold and escalate the proposed activity rather than proceeding.

## Relationship to prior work

Sprint 12 depends on the completed planning boundaries for billing and pilot
operations, data-path/recovery, security/compliance, and operations/DR. Those
boundaries do not supply actual evidence or authorization. The Phase 5
biometric, consent, evaluation, human-oversight, and model-release gates remain
unchanged.
