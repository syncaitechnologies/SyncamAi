# United States privacy supplement

> Draft operational privacy policy — requires review and approval by qualified legal/privacy counsel before production use in the applicable jurisdiction.

Version: `2026-09-08.draft-1`. Checked: 2026-09-08. Read with the [global policy](global-privacy-policy.md). Sources US-01–US-10 and state/local applicability are in the [matrix](privacy-jurisdiction-matrix.md).

## Federal and state layers

There is no single comprehensive federal privacy rule that makes this national notice sufficient. FTC Act section 5 prohibits applicable unfair/deceptive practices; the FTC's biometric policy describes concerns including unsupported accuracy claims, unexpected collection, inadequate risk assessment and safeguards, discriminatory harms and failures to oversee vendors. Claims must match tested behavior. Sectoral laws require separate evaluation: COPPA for covered children's online services, FERPA for qualifying education records, HIPAA for qualifying entities/data, and communications/workplace rules for audio or employment use. They do not all apply merely because a camera exists. No regulated-sector deployment is approved here.

For each site record the state/locality, affected resident/worker populations, purposes, customers' and SyncCam's roles, revenue/processing thresholds, exemptions and any biometric-specific coverage independent of general privacy thresholds. Unknown jurisdictions remain blocked. The initial matrix evaluates California, Illinois, Texas, Washington, Colorado, NYC and Portland as material examples; it is not a 50-state clearance. Additional launch states must be assessed from current official law before deployment.

## California

When CCPA/CPRA applies, provide notice at collection and a policy reflecting actual categories, sources, purposes, recipient categories, retention criteria and applicable sensitive information. Evaluate employee/workplace information as well as consumers; do not assume the former employee exemption persists. Support applicable access/knowledge, deletion, correction, sale/sharing opt-out, sensitive-information limitation and non-discrimination rights, including authorized-agent processes. Document service-provider/contractor restrictions where those roles apply.

CPPA rules effective 1 January 2026 add material risk-assessment, cybersecurity and ADMT requirements with different compliance dates. The approved text provides ADMT compliance from 1 January 2027 for covered significant decisions and phased first cybersecurity audit reports in 2028–2030; do not treat every deadline as 2026. Have counsel determine applicability and assessment timing before deployment. No payroll or disciplinary automation is authorized.

No advertising or sale/sharing implementation was found, but vendor and contractual analysis is incomplete. Do not publish an unconditional no-sale/sharing statement until verified. If regulated sale/sharing is introduced, implement required opt-out mechanisms/signals before use; biometric sale remains prohibited by product policy and may be forbidden by statute.

## Biometric and local restrictions

Illinois BIPA requires pre-collection written information/release, a public retention/destruction policy, restrictions on disclosure and commercial use, and protective handling. Destruction follows purpose completion or three years after the last interaction, whichever is earlier, subject to the statute. Do not convert this outer limit into a universal three-year default. Employees are not categorically excluded.

Texas CUBI addresses consent before commercial capture, disclosure, protection and destruction; its purpose-linked outside limit differs from Illinois. Washington's statute has particular enrollment/commercial-purpose definitions and exclusions that require analysis rather than copying BIPA language. Colorado's biometric amendments, effective July 2025, add biometric policies/consent and employment-specific limitations. NYC regulates covered commercial establishments' collection of customers' biometric identifiers, including signage and sale/profit restrictions. Portland prohibits private facial recognition in public accommodations subject to defined exceptions. A generic consent form cannot override a prohibition.

Before any face deployment, counsel must determine coverage under each relevant state/local rule, approve necessity and consent/alternatives, and reconcile retention, access, correction, deletion, disclosure, security and incident response. The [biometric checklist](../governance/biometric-attendance-approval-evidence-checklist.md) is authoritative. No face-recognition implementation, model promotion, customer enrollment or U.S. activation is approved by this package.

## Rights and incident handling

Use the [request procedure](data-subject-request-procedure.md) for proportionate verification and human-reviewed fulfillment, including applicable opt-out/limitation, profiling objections and appeals. Do not require unnecessary identity documents for requests that legally need no verification. State security/breach requirements have different data definitions, harm tests, timing and regulator thresholds. The incident lead and counsel must determine these from affected populations; there is no nationwide 72-hour clock. These operational processes remain to be implemented and staffed.
