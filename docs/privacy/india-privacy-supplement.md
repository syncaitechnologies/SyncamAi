# India privacy supplement

> Draft operational privacy policy — requires review and approval by qualified legal/privacy counsel before production use in the applicable jurisdiction.

Version: `2026-09-08.draft-1`. Checked: 2026-09-08. Read with the [global policy](global-privacy-policy.md). Primary instruments are recorded as IN-01–IN-06 in the [source matrix](privacy-jurisdiction-matrix.md).

## Commencement as checked

The Act is enacted; its core processing obligations are not all operative. G.S.R. 843(E), dated 13 November 2025, provides:

| Stage | Act provisions | Position on 2026-09-08 |
|---|---|---|
| Gazette publication | Section 1(2), 2, 18–26, 35, 38–43, 44(1)/(3) | Operative |
| One year from publication | 6(9), 27(1)(d) | Scheduled 13 November 2026 |
| Eighteen months from publication | 3–5, 6(1)–(8)/(10), 7–17, remainder of 27, 28–34, 36–37, 44(2) | Scheduled 13 May 2027 |

Rules 1, 2 and 17–21 commence on publication; rule 4 after one year; rules 3, 5–16, 22–23 after eighteen months. Use the Gazette date, not MeitY's 14 November web posting date. Read the 10 December 2025 corrigendum with the Rules. Recheck notifications before deployment; this is not a statement that every provision became effective in 2025.

## Operational design toward applicable obligations

The customer usually acts as Data Fiduciary for workplace/camera purposes; SyncCam usually acts as contracted Data Processor. Independent SyncCam purposes need separate role assessment. Design an itemized notice listing account, camera/device, event/audit and any separately approved attendance categories, a specific purpose for each, recipients, contact and available rights. Make notices understandable and accessible, including language choices appropriate to the deployment. The Data Fiduciary must supervise processors rather than treating a contract as discharge of its duties.

Record the selected ground per purpose. Section 7 includes specified legitimate uses, including an employment-related provision; it is not a blanket authorization for workplace biometrics. Counsel must assess its scope, necessity and applicable employment rules. DPDP does not create a separate statutory sensitive-personal-data category; SyncCam's enhanced biometric controls remain product safeguards. Current IT Act/SPDI rules separately matter, including passwords and biometric information, until applicable transition changes. Do not remove existing protections in anticipation of future commencement.

The implementation plan must support informed consent, equally accessible withdrawal, access information, correction/updating, erasure, grievance and nomination when applicable. Withdrawal must stop future consent-based processing and trigger reviewed deletion instructions to processors; lawful retention exceptions must be identified rather than silently treating withdrawal as failure. Assign the request/grievance owner and approved response period. No request service is implemented yet.

Security, breach handling, category retention, processor contracts and deletion verification are release prerequisites. The Rules introduce particular security/processing-record retention duties, including one-year requirements: counsel must reconcile these with purpose-based erasure and ADR-006 before selecting a data-category schedule. The three-year inactivity rule for specified classes is not a universal SyncCam retention policy. See the [incident clocks](privacy-incident-and-breach-procedure.md), including distinct CERT-In duties that must be evaluated now.

Cross-border review must consider DPDP section 16, rule 15, any current government restrictions and sector-specific duties. Do not describe a universal country whitelist or promise localization merely because a tenant row names a region. Evaluate children under 18, verifiable parental consent and prohibited child tracking/behavioral monitoring where applicable; a general CCTV notice is insufficient. No child-directed deployment or biometric program is activated.

## Separate biometric/attendance gate

Future notices must identify facial images, templates/embeddings, liveness signals, employee identifiers, attendance events and consent evidence separately, with purpose, recipients, necessity, retention and withdrawal treatment for each. Require an actual non-biometric alternative, tenant/site isolation, encrypted template handling, deletion/backup reconciliation and human correction of disputed outcomes. Employer legal responsibilities and SyncCam's processor responsibilities both need review. The [biometric checklist](../governance/biometric-attendance-approval-evidence-checklist.md) still blocks implementation and activation; none of these records are created by this draft.

Before use, fill the legal entity and grievance contact, approve a jurisdiction/purpose determination and PIA, staff the rights/incident procedure, and verify each promised control through [traceability](privacy-implementation-traceability.md).
