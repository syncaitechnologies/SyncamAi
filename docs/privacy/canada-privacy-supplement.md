# Canada privacy supplement

> Draft operational privacy policy — requires review and approval by qualified legal/privacy counsel before production use in the applicable jurisdiction.

Version: `2026-09-08.draft-1`. Checked: 2026-09-08. Read with the [global policy](global-privacy-policy.md) and CA-01–CA-09 in the [source matrix](privacy-jurisdiction-matrix.md).

## Determine jurisdiction first

PIPEDA does not govern every Canadian deployment. Determine the organization, province, public/private sector, federally regulated status, employment/consumer context, and interprovincial/international flows. Alberta PIPA, BC PIPA and Quebec's private-sector Act may govern intra-provincial private-sector processing; PIPEDA can still govern commercial cross-border transactions and federal works, undertakings or businesses. PIPEDA employment coverage is tied to federally regulated organizations. Provincial employment, labour, human-rights and other duties may apply even where PIPEDA does not. Public-sector, health and school deployments require separate assessment.

## Proposed operational protections

Assign an accountable privacy officer and publish an approved contact. Record purposes before collection. Provide understandable meaningful consent where required, minimize collection, limit use/disclosure/retention, maintain accuracy, protect information, explain practices openly, and provide access/correction and a complaint/challenge route. These fair-information principles guide the design; the [traceability](privacy-implementation-traceability.md) identifies missing controls.

Customers choosing workplace surveillance must document necessity, proportionality, affected people, camera placement and less intrusive alternatives. SyncCam must document its own processing and contractually control subprocessors. The roles are not automatically joint controllers. Express opt-in is the default product requirement for sensitive/biometric use unless qualified Canadian counsel approves a different lawful basis for the specific deployment; an employer's preference is insufficient.

Before requesting consent, explain precisely what is collected, the specific purpose, who receives it, meaningful risks/consequences, retention, withdrawal and the practical alternative. Keep this separate from general terms. No preselected biometric checkbox, coerced acceptance or bundled attendance permission. Use the [separate proposed notice](biometric-and-face-recognition-notice.md); it does not enable collection.

## Province-specific decisions

- Alberta: assess PIPA scope and its employee-information rules, reasonable purposes, notices, access/correction, service providers outside Canada, and breach requirements. A permitted employee-information exception is not universal biometric approval.
- British Columbia: assess PIPA's employee-information rules, withdrawal, access/correction and section 35 retention, including information used to make a decision directly affecting a person. A universal 30-day deletion default would conflict with some required retention. Review current breach guidance and other applicable laws.
- Quebec: apply the private-sector Act as modernized by Law 25, including governance, privacy officer, sensitive consent, technology-project PIA, cross-border assessment, confidentiality incidents, rights/portability and applicable automated-decision transparency. The technology-law biometric provisions separately require disclosure of identity-verification uses and disclosure of a biometric bank at least 60 days before service. Express consent and an alternative method remain essential. A filing is not proof that a deployment is approved or lawful.

The [incident procedure](privacy-incident-and-breach-procedure.md) distinguishes PIPEDA, Alberta and Quebec triggers and clocks; it deliberately corrects the old blanket 72-hour statement. Rights responses, exceptions and extensions must be assigned by applicable law and tracked by a human owner. A processing inventory and approved schedules must precede any erasure promise.

## Publication blockers

Identify the actual employer/customer jurisdiction and SyncCam role; approve necessity/PIA, exact notice and consent language, language/accessibility needs, transfer/contracts, retention, deletion and complaint ownership. Demonstrate safeguards and an operational non-biometric alternative. Keep biometric enrollment, matching, liveness and attendance disabled pending every existing gate. No Canadian compliance approval is asserted.
